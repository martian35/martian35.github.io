Chronological Ordering of Shuffled Images

SNU AI Challenge · GeekSeek · 2026 · 8th Place

Chronological ordering of four shuffled frames using a LoRA-adapted vision-language model and constrained permutation decoding.

CodeChallenge
Overview / Methodology / Results / My contribution

Overview

Input: a sentence and four shuffled frames. Output: the chronological rank of each input frame.

Methodology

01 Vision-language adaptation

Adapt Qwen3.5-27B with LoRA. Replace free-form answers with structured readouts.

  • Pairwise evidence: Six readouts compare the temporal order of frame pairs.
  • Slot evidence: Four readouts score the chronological positions.
  • Training signals: Temporal-gap supervision and hard-negative ranking support the final training stage.

Sources: Structured readout and training

02 Constrained permutation decoding

Combine pair and slot evidence. Score all 24 valid chronological orders.

  • Decoding: Select the highest-scoring permutation. Each frame appears exactly once.
  • Output conversion: Convert the ordered sequence into per-input ranks. These are inverse permutations.

Sources: Rank and chronological-order conversion · Structured readout and training

03 Random4 inference

Evaluate four input-image orderings. Align their outputs before aggregation.

  • Frame identity: Map each view’s logits back to the original image identities.
  • Aggregation: Average the aligned logits. Apply the decoder to the combined evidence.
  • Validation: Test permutation mappings and the final submission format.

Sources: Canonical aggregation · Permutation regression checks

04 Snapshot averaging

Average nine language-LoRA snapshots from one training trajectory. Accumulate parameters in CPU float32.

  • Inference artifact: The result is one adapter. Parameter averaging adds no forward passes.
  • Inference cost: Random4 still requires four input-order evaluations.

Sources: Exp43 Full-SWA · Post-competition boundary
