Accelerated MRI Reconstruction

SNU FastMRI Challenge · Team 2rm1000 · 2025 · 3rd Place

MRI reconstruction from undersampled k-space using Feature/FI VarNet, acquisition-specific experts, and model averaging.

Method

  • Reconstruction. Feature VarNet and FI VarNet combine learned updates with multi-coil sensitivity estimation and data consistency.
  • Specialization. Route brain/knee scans to 4× or 8× experts using coil count and sampling-mask information.
  • Training and inference. Use MRI-aware augmentation, memory-efficient training, checkpoint averaging, and prediction ensembling.

Measurement-consistent reconstruction

The input is undersampled multi-coil k-space, so plausible appearance alone is insufficient: a reconstruction must remain tied to the acquired measurements. The Feature VarNet and FI VarNet implementations estimate coil sensitivities and combine learned updates with data-consistency operations. FI VarNet also includes image-domain cascades. The design couples a learned image prior to the MRI acquisition model.

Sources: Reconstruction models

Specialization with explicit routing

Brain and knee scans at 4× and 8× acceleration present different reconstruction conditions. The team trained specialized experts for these cases. At inference, coil count identifies the anatomy and the sampling mask determines acceleration, providing a deterministic route to the appropriate model.

Sources: Expert routing and inference

Making the training pipeline practical

The repository provides DeepSpeed-based training, activation-checkpointing configuration, resumable checkpoints, and conversion of distributed checkpoint state into FP32 model weights. MRI-aware augmentation and a warmup/cosine schedule support training. These pieces matter because a useful reconstruction architecture must also fit the available memory and survive interrupted experiments.

Sources: Training and checkpoint handling · Training strategy

Two different forms of averaging

Checkpoint averaging combines compatible parameter tensors into one model; prediction ensembling instead executes separate models and averages their reconstructions. The averaging utility checks parameter names and shapes, while the inference path supports expert-specific model pairs. Keeping these mechanisms separate makes the inference-cost trade-off explicit: weight averaging does not add another model forward pass.

Sources: Parameter averaging · Prediction ensembling

Result and engineering takeaway

Team 2rm1000, with Jinseok Choi, placed third and received a KRW 2,500,000 team prize. The submission brought together measurement-aware models, anatomy-specific inference, and reusable checkpoints in a complete reconstruction pipeline.
