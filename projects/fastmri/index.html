Accelerated MRI Reconstruction

SNU FastMRI Challenge · Team 2rm1000 · 2025 · 3rd out of 183 teams

Reconstruct undersampled MRI with acquisition-specific Feature/FI VarNet models and checkpoint averaging.

CodeChallenge2025 final leaderboardOfficial coverage2025 announcementPresentation

Problem

Reconstruct brain and knee images from 4× or 8× undersampled multi-coil k-space. The challenge limits inference to 3,600 seconds on a GTX 1080 with 8GB VRAM.

  • Measurement model: Retain the acquired frequency samples while learning to correct reconstruction artifacts.
  • Design choice: Specialize models by anatomy and acceleration, then allocate ensemble computation by condition.

My contribution

I worked on Fourier-domain modeling with Jinseok Choi in Team 2rm1000. The model pipeline and competition submission were team work.

Reconstruction method

1. Rule-based expert routing

Determine the acquisition condition before running its reconstruction model.

  • Anatomy: Treat 15-coil inputs as knee scans under this challenge’s data convention; route other inputs to brain models.
  • Acceleration: Remove the contiguous calibration region from the sampling-density calculation. Classify the remaining density as 4× or 8×.
  • Selection: Use these explicit rules to select the configured model or model pair.

2. Feature-domain reconstruction and data consistency

Estimate coil sensitivities, combine the coil images, and encode them into learned features.

  • Feature VarNet: Run 12 feature cascades with 32 feature channels and an 8-channel sensitivity network.
  • FI VarNet: Follow the 12 feature cascades with six image-domain cascades.
  • Weight sharing: Share the feature encoder and decoder across cascades. Keep the cascade U-Nets separate and disable attention.
  • Measurement residual: Decode the features into predicted k-space. Compute disagreement only at acquired samples and encode that residual back into features.
  • Cascade update: Subtract the learned correction and the weighted measurement-residual correction. Repeat before coil combination and output cropping.

r_k = M ⊙ (k_pred − y)

M is the acquisition mask, y is measured multi-coil k-space, and k_pred is the current prediction. Each cascade uses this residual for a learned-weight data-consistency update.

3. Acquisition-aware data processing

Prepare training examples for the target anatomy and acceleration.

  • Mask adaptation: Rebuild the mask for the chosen 4× or 8× setting before applying it to training k-space.
  • MRI augmentation: Transform the full and masked coil images together, then recompute k-space and the reconstruction target.
  • Schedule: Use small rotations, shearing, and horizontal flips. Increase augmentation strength with the exponential schedule shown in the presentation.
  • Loss region: Build an anatomy-specific target mask with thresholding, erosion, and dilation. Mask the prediction and target before computing SSIM loss.
  • Final fit: The submission scripts pool the supplied training and validation splits for final training.

4. Training within the memory budget

Combine activation checkpointing with DeepSpeed optimizer-state offloading where enabled.

  • Checkpointed cascades: Recompute intermediate activations during backpropagation instead of keeping every cascade’s activations in GPU memory.
  • CPU offload: The DeepSpeed path uses CPU Adam and offloads optimizer state to the CPU.
  • Specialization: Resume the shared-acceleration brain models for 4× or 8× fine-tuning. Train separate knee experts.
  • Learning rate: Use warmup followed by cosine scheduling.

5. Checkpoint averaging and inference

Average compatible checkpoints before combining model outputs.

  • Parameter average: Load checkpoint tensors on the CPU, average them in float32, and save one model state.
  • Inference cost: Weight averaging retains one forward pass per model. Output ensembling runs both selected models and averages their reconstructions.
  • Final output: Collect reconstructed slices by volume and write them in slice order for submission.
