Engineering decisions

A useful map must explain more than its training images

3D Gaussian Splatting represents a scene as many small, oriented 3D elements that can be rendered into an image. My research asks how that representation can support mapping: which observations to optimize, how to use geometric evidence, and how to spend computation where the reconstruction still needs work.

One concrete engineering problem: coordinate consistency

In my public Aria-to-3DGS pipeline, stereo-inertial tracking exports camera poses, map points, and observations into the same raw map frame before conversion to a reconstruction dataset. Mixing those map points with a separately normalized trajectory would silently break their spatial relationship. Sparse depth targets, consistency checks, and ablation tools make this interface explicit.

What I am investigating now

Current experiments examine how optimization effort is allocated across views and how depth or free-space evidence can constrain spurious geometry. These are research questions under evaluation. The work is ongoing, and I am preparing a manuscript; results across scenes and computational trade-offs still need broader validation.

How this connects to my current research

This work studies how a 3D representation is built and checked. At AIDAS Lab, I explore the next step: using reconstructed geometry to create robot-learning data.

Research direction

This ongoing project investigates the use of 3D Gaussian Splatting in simultaneous localization and mapping. The goal is to connect camera motion estimation with a 3D representation of the surrounding scene.

Current status

The manuscript is in preparation. Further method details, experimental results, and publication information will be shared as the work progresses.

Public implementation thread

The Aria-to-3DGS repository contains tracking exports, dataset conversion, sparse-depth target generation, consistency checks, and mapping ablation scripts. It is a supporting pipeline within the broader mapping research.
