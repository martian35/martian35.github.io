Overview

A physical power curve supplies the baseline, LightGBM learns its residual error, and Optuna tunes a multi-model forecast ensemble.

Input
Weather observations and derived atmospheric features for three wind farms.
Output
Hourly power-generation forecasts, evaluated by prediction error and the competition’s settlement rule.

Methodology

01 Physical baseline from weather features

The turbine power curve provides an expected generation level. Weather-derived features, including air-density estimation informed by the Arden–Buck equation, supply the physical context.

Model structure
Let a physical relationship explain the baseline before introducing learned corrections.
Forecast setting
Generate hourly predictions for Gyeongju, Yangyang, and Yeongdeok.

02 Residual learning with LightGBM

A LightGBM model predicts the remaining difference between the physical baseline and observed generation. Add that correction to the baseline to form the hybrid forecast.

Learning target
Fit the baseline’s residual error rather than replacing the entire physical model.
Hybrid prediction
Carry the physical baseline and learned error correction into the ensemble.

hybrid forecast = physical power-curve estimate + learned residual

03 Ensemble optimization and evaluation

Blend the hybrid forecast with Ridge Regression and Random Forest predictions. Use Optuna to tune model hyperparameters and ensemble weights.

Optimization

Implementation notes & source evidence

Two objectives for an operational forecast

The competition required hourly generation forecasts for Gyeongju, Yangyang, and Yeongdeok. Prediction error and settlement revenue describe different aspects of usefulness, so the project reported both. A forecast that looks numerically close must still be evaluated under the competition’s economic scoring rule.

Let physics explain the baseline

The team used a physical power curve and weather-derived features, including air-density estimation informed by the Arden–Buck equation. This gives the model a physical starting point: weather conditions imply an expected generation level. A residual LightGBM model then corrects the baseline’s remaining error instead of making the entire mapping depend on a single unconstrained predictor.

Sources: Team architecture slide

My contribution: architecture and optimization

I worked on model architecture design and hyperparameter optimization. Validation and engineering takeaway

The key design lesson is to divide the problem between a physical baseline, learned error correction, and the operational evaluation criterion.
